\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Learned symmetry in trained networks.}
Equivariance can be built into an architecture, as in group-equivariant CNNs~\citep{cohen2016group}, or learned from
data. Networks trained on natural images contain families of units related by a transformation, such as rotated curve
detectors and mirrored pairs of units~\citep{olah2020naturally}, and their representations can be measured for
equivariance directly~\citep{lenc2015understanding,gruver2023lie}. \citet{bokman2023investigating} found evidence
that CNNs trained with flip augmentation are close to group-equivariant CNNs whose channels permute under a flip, and
merged such a network with its own mirrored copy. \citet{shahverdi2026identifiable} prove that, under identifiability
conditions, an equivariant network must be equivariant layer by layer. We study released vision transformers, where
the residual stream needs a learned mirror operator with odd directions rather than a channel permutation, we compare
four symmetries in the same models, and we turn the structure into a converted network.

\paragraph{Making pretrained models equivariant.}
Equivariance can be added to a pretrained model by averaging over the group at inference or during
fine-tuning~\citep{basu2023equi,basu2023efficient}, by learning a canonicalisation of the
input~\citep{kaba2023equivariance,mondal2023equivariant}, or by projecting the weights onto a designed subspace with
bounded, soft equivariance~\citep{rahman2026tunable}. Group averaging is exact but multiplies the inference cost by
the group size. Mirror Surgery is exact at the cost of one forward pass, and it estimates the mirror operator and the
pairing of units from the model's own activations. Mirror-equivariant vision transformers can also be trained from
scratch: \citet{bokman2025flopping} split features into mirror-symmetric and antisymmetric parts, which makes linear
layers block-diagonal and halves their FLOPs with measured speed-ups; \citet{nordstrom2025quick} extend this to larger
groups. We use the same even and odd decomposition, but obtain it by converting released models rather than by
training from scratch.

\paragraph{Structured compression.}
Post-training compression of transformers factorises weights at low rank (plain SVD, ASVD~\citep{yuan2023asvd},
SVD-LLM~\citep{wang2025svdllm}), slices a rotated residual stream~\citep{ashkboos2024slicegpt}, prunes
structures~\citep{an2024flap} or merges correlated units~\citep{stoica2024zipit}; token merging~\citep{bolya2023tome}
reduces tokens instead. These methods use activation statistics. Twins respond to mirrored inputs, so they are almost
uncorrelated on any single image and invisible to correlation-based merging.
