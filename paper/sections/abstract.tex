\begin{abstract}
Many MLP neurons in pretrained vision transformers come in mirror pairs: one neuron detects a pattern and its twin
detects the mirror image of that pattern. We measure this duplication, which we call the symmetry tax, in 14
released vision models. It is concentrated in the first half of the network, where it is about as strong for up-down
flips and 180-degree rotations as for left-right flips, much weaker for 90-degree rotations, and present even in
models trained without flips, so early pairing follows the symmetries of natural images rather than the training
augmentation. We then introduce Mirror Surgery, a closed-form conversion of a pretrained transformer into a network
that is exactly equivariant to left-right mirroring and stores each twin pair once. A counting formula from
representation theory gives its parameter count exactly; for DINOv2-B it keeps 76.5\% of the parameters. After a
short distillation, the converted model reaches 78.34\% ImageNet $k$-NN accuracy against 78.06\% for SVD-LLM at the
same size (three seeds each) and leads it on linear probing, four transfer datasets and ADE20K segmentation; unlike
every compressor we compare with, its prediction never changes when the image is mirrored. It gives exact flip invariance in
one forward pass, at twice the throughput of test-time flip averaging and 0.66 points below it, and it separates
what an object is (even features) from which way it faces (odd features). Before healing, however, Mirror Surgery is
weaker than SVD-LLM. All experiments were pre-registered, and we report the predictions that failed.
\end{abstract}
