\begin{abstract}
Many MLP neurons in pretrained vision transformers come in mirror pairs: one neuron detects a pattern and its twin
detects the mirror image of that pattern. We measure this duplication, which we call the symmetry tax, in 14 released
vision models. It is concentrated in the first half of the network. There it is about as strong for up-down flips as
for left-right flips, much weaker for 90-degree rotations, and of similar strength in two models trained without
flips, which suggests that early pairing reflects the symmetries of natural images more than the training
augmentation. We then present Mirror Surgery, a training-free conversion of a pretrained ViT into a network that is
exactly equivariant to left-right mirroring, followed by a short distillation. In even and odd coordinates the
converted DINOv2-B (with registers) has 23.5\% fewer parameters, a number given exactly by a standard count of equivariant linear
maps. After healing it matches SVD-LLM at the same size on ImageNet $k$-NN (78.34 against 78.06, three seeds each;
paired 95\% interval of the difference $+0.09$ to $+0.47$) and on linear probing, four transfer datasets and ADE20K,
and in float32 its prediction never changes when an image is mirrored. It gives flip invariance in one forward pass,
0.66 points below two-pass test-time flip averaging, and separates what an object is (even features) from which way
it faces (odd features). Two limits matter: before healing Mirror Surgery is weaker than SVD-LLM, and our even/odd
implementation is slower than the dense original. All experiments were pre-registered; we report every prediction,
including the 16 that failed.
\end{abstract}
