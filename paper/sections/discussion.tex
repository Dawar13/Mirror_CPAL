\section{Limitations and outlook}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What did not work.}
Before healing, Mirror Surgery loses to SVD-LLM at every size on DINOv2-B/14 with registers and on the first half of
eight models, and collapses on SigLIP~2 (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}). Stacking low rank inside the even and odd blocks
never beat low rank alone, and a rule fixed in advance to predict, from weights alone, which method hurts a block less
was right on 67\% of 48 held-out blocks, short of its 75\% target. Our controlled test of the cause of the pairing could
not be run: small ViTs trained from scratch on ImageNet-100, with and without flips, formed too few strong twins, and
the study stopped at its pre-registered gate.

\paragraph{Scope.}
All healed results are for one model at one size, and the three healing seeds share one fitted structure. The smaller
even/odd form runs at 0.72 of the original's throughput in our implementation, so the pre-registered speed bar of 1.1
times the original is not met; the fast equivariant ViTs of \citet{bokman2025flopping} suggest that better kernels could
change this. The conversion needs GELU MLPs and learned position embeddings, so 4 of our 14 models cannot be converted
as is, and the converted model is tied to its position grid (higher resolutions need it re-projected, as we did for
ADE20K). Exact invariance holds in float32; bfloat16 rounding breaks it for 0.27\% of images.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Early blocks are about as close to up-down as to left-right symmetry, so converting them for the group generated by both
flips~\citep{nordstrom2025quick} could remove more parameters, and training with 90-degree rotations would test our
explanation of early pairing.
