\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Our main model is DINOv2-B/14 with registers~\citep{oquab2024dinov2,darcet2024registers} at $224$ pixels. All
structures are fitted on 2{,}000 ImageNet training images~\citep{deng2009imagenet}; healing uses a disjoint set of
100{,}000 training images, and hyperparameters are chosen on further disjoint training images. Validation images are
used only for reporting. Our main metric is weighted $k$-NN top-1 accuracy of the class-token
feature~\citep{wu2018unsupervised}, with $k=20$ and temperature $0.07$ as in DINO~\citep{caron2021emerging}, a bank of
100{,}000 training images and all 50{,}000 validation images as queries. \emph{Flip consistency} is the share of validation images for which the $k$-NN
prediction is the same as for the mirrored image, computed in float32. We compare against plain SVD,
SVD-LLM~\citep{wang2025svdllm}, activation-aware scaling as in ASVD~\citep{yuan2023asvd},
SliceGPT~\citep{ashkboos2024slicegpt}, FLAP~\citep{an2024flap} and ZipIt~\citep{stoica2024zipit}, applied to the same
matrices. Every baseline is matched to Mirror Surgery's parameter count to within 1\% (in practice within 0.2\%), and
its hyperparameters are chosen on 10{,}000 held-out training images. Two controls use Mirror Surgery's machinery with
a broken structure: a random mirror operator with the same number of odd directions and a random pairing of the same
size (\emph{random structure}), and pairings chosen by same-image correlation or by weight similarity instead of by the
mirror. All healed models use the recipe of Section~\ref{sec:method}, with the same steps, data and learning-rate
selection; the baselines keep their own structure (fixed rank, slice width or pruned width) while they heal. The two
strongest methods, Mirror Surgery and SVD-LLM, were healed with three seeds; the other methods once. All runs used one
NVIDIA H200 GPU.

\paragraph{Main result.}
Table~\ref{tab:healed} compares the healed full models. Mirror Surgery reaches 78.34 $k$-NN top-1 (three seeds,
standard deviation 0.02), against 78.06 for SVD-LLM, the baseline that came first in our pre-registered selection, and
0.61 below the uncompressed original. It is the only compressed model that is exactly flip-invariant: the other
compressors disagree with themselves on 4 to 6\% of mirrored validation images, and so does the original model on
3.7\%. The comparison is fair in the sense that matters for the claim: same parameter count, same data, same number of
healing steps and the same learning-rate rule. The random-structure control, which has exactly the same shapes and is
also exactly invariant, ends 8.4 points lower, so the gain comes from the fitted structure and not from the
equivariant form or the healing alone. Healing is essential: the converted model starts at 53.84, far below SVD-LLM's
75.80, and is ahead of SVD-LLM from the first evaluation at step 100 onwards (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}). The three healing seeds share one fitted structure,
so their spread does not include the variation of the fit.

\paragraph{Exact invariance at the cost of one pass.}
The usual way to make a model flip-invariant is to average its features over the image and its mirror image. On the
original model this gives 79.00, but it needs two forward passes, which halves throughput (Table~\ref{tab:healed}).
Healed Mirror Surgery is 0.66 points below flip averaging (paired bootstrap over the 50{,}000 images for seed 0:
$-0.68$, 95\% interval $-0.87$ to $-0.51$) with one pass. Stored with dense projected weights, it has the architecture
of the original and runs at the same speed, about twice the throughput of flip averaging. The 23.5\% parameter saving
is realised only in the even/odd form of Section~\ref{sec:method}, which in our implementation uses slightly less
memory but runs at 0.73 of the original's throughput, so we make no speed claim for it; the low-rank baselines are
also slower than the original in the same benchmark. Mirror Surgery composes with other efficiency methods: int8
weight quantisation costs it 0.006 points (original: 0.004), and token merging~\citep{bolya2023tome} 0.18 points
(original: 0.34).

\paragraph{Downstream tasks.}
The $k$-NN advantage carries over to every downstream task we tried (Table~\ref{tab:downstream}). With frozen
features, healed Mirror Surgery is ahead of healed SVD-LLM on the ImageNet linear probe (80.11 against 79.72), on all
four transfer datasets~\citep{krizhevsky2009learning,cimpoi2014describing,parkhi2012cats,nilsback2008automated}, and on
ADE20K semantic segmentation~\citep{zhou2017scene} (47.16 against 46.79 mIoU, 0.43 below the original). Under
distribution shift, its accuracy drop from ImageNet to ImageNet-V2~\citep{recht2019imagenet} is 9.17 points against
8.62 for the original, within the one-point margin we pre-registered, and smaller than SVD-LLM's 9.42. On
ImageNet-Sketch~\citep{wang2019learning} it leads SVD-LLM by 4.2 points, but this number should be read with care:
the dataset fills some classes with flipped and rotated copies of other images, which an exactly invariant model
treats alike. For segmentation the converted backbone is exactly equivariant, and a head symmetrised with $A^\star$
gives the same mask, mirrored, for 99.9999\% of the 487.7 million pixels of a mirrored image (the original model:
95.0\%).

\paragraph{Before healing.}
Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot} shows the conversion without any training. Mirror Surgery on the first six blocks loses only
1.6 points, and the structure is clearly what matters: pairing the same neurons by same-image correlation or by weight
similarity, with everything else unchanged, falls 37 to 49 points below Mirror Surgery, and a random structure
destroys the model. This
matches the twin statistics: a twin pair responds to mirrored inputs, so its two neurons are almost uncorrelated on any
single image (median same-token correlation 0.05), and correlation-based merging cannot find them. SVD-LLM, however,
is better than Mirror Surgery at every size, by 1.8 points on six MLPs and by 22 points on the full model, where the
late blocks, with few odd directions, are hurt by the projection. Mirror Surgery is therefore a conversion followed by
a short heal, and its value lies in what the healed model is (exactly invariant, competitive at equal size, and
interpretable, below), not in a better zero-shot compression.

\paragraph{What the even and odd parts encode.}
An exactly invariant feature cannot know which way an object faces, and an odd feature changes sign when the image
is mirrored. In the healed model this splits the representation cleanly (Table~\ref{tab:cub}). On CUB-200-2011
birds~\citep{wah2011caltech}, the even part of the class token identifies the species as well as the full feature
(89.47 against 89.42), but a probe trained to read the facing direction from it is exactly at chance on the
mirror-augmented test set, because every image and its mirror image receive the same prediction. The odd part, which
holds 68 of 768 directions, predicts the facing direction at 99.2\% and the species at 3.7\%. The original model
mixes the two: its even part still knows the facing direction (86.7\%), and its odd part knows something about the
species (25.0\%). Mirror Surgery therefore gives, at no extra cost, a feature for what is in the image and a separate
small feature for which way it is oriented.
