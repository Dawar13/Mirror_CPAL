\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Our main model is DINOv2-B/14 with registers~\citep{oquab2024dinov2,darcet2024registers} at $224$ pixels. Structures
are fitted on 2{,}000 ImageNet training images~\citep{deng2009imagenet}; healing uses a disjoint set of 100{,}000
training images, and all choices are made on further disjoint training images. Validation images are used only for
reporting. The main metric is weighted $k$-NN top-1 accuracy of the class-token feature~\citep{wu2018unsupervised},
with $k=20$ and temperature $0.07$ as in DINO~\citep{caron2021emerging}, a bank of 100{,}000 training images (a subset,
so accuracies are below published full-bank numbers) and all 50{,}000 validation images as queries. Accuracies use
bfloat16 features; \emph{flip consistency}, the share of validation images whose prediction does not change when the
image is mirrored, uses float32. The original model scores 78.95 in the healing pipeline and 78.94 in the compression
pipeline of Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot}.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare with plain SVD, SVD-LLM~\citep{wang2025svdllm} (its whitening step, without the sequential update),
activation-aware scaling as in ASVD~\citep{yuan2023asvd}, and simplified versions of SliceGPT~\citep{ashkboos2024slicegpt},
FLAP~\citep{an2024flap} and ZipIt~\citep{stoica2024zipit} (merging within one model), all applied to the same
matrices and matched to Mirror Surgery's parameter count from above to within 0.25\%. Hyperparameters are chosen by
$k$-NN accuracy on 10{,}000 held-out training images. Two kinds of control use Mirror Surgery's machinery with the
wrong structure: a random mirror operator with a random pairing of the same sizes (\emph{random structure}), and
pairings chosen by same-image correlation or by weight similarity instead of by the mirror. As pre-registered, we
healed the two best baselines on the held-out images (SVD-LLM, then SliceGPT), plain SVD and the random control, with
the recipe of Section~\ref{sec:method}: the same data, steps, flip-averaged final targets and learning-rate rule. The
baselines keep their own structure while healing (fixed rank, slice width or pruned width), and their block targets
are the teacher's block outputs, where Mirror Surgery's are symmetrised with $A^\star$. Every method chose the largest
learning rate of the grid; a check at twice that rate would not have won for Mirror Surgery or SVD-LLM. Mirror
Surgery and SVD-LLM were healed with three seeds each, which share one fitted structure. All runs used one NVIDIA H200.

\paragraph{Main result.}
Table~\ref{tab:healed} compares the healed full models. Mirror Surgery reaches 78.34 $k$-NN top-1 (three seeds,
standard deviation 0.02) and SVD-LLM, the baseline ranked first on the held-out images, 78.06; the pre-registered
test was non-inferiority, and it passes. A paired bootstrap over the validation images, which we added afterwards,
gives a difference of $+0.28$ points (95\% interval $+0.09$ to $+0.47$; the interval excludes zero for each seed
pair too), so Mirror Surgery matches SVD-LLM and is slightly better. This interval covers image and healing-seed
variation but not the variation of the fitted structure. Mirror Surgery is also the only compressed model that is
exactly flip-invariant: the other compressors change their prediction for 4 to 6\% of validation images when the image
is mirrored, and the original model does so for 3.7\%. The random-structure control, which has exactly the same shapes
and is also exactly invariant, ends 8.4 points lower, so the gain comes from the fitted structure and not from the
equivariant form or from healing alone. Healing is essential: the converted model starts at 53.84, far below
SVD-LLM's 75.80, and is ahead of it from the first check after step 0 (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}).

\paragraph{Invariance and cost.}
The usual way to make a model flip-invariant is to average its features over the image and its mirror image. On the
original model this gives 79.00 but needs two forward passes, which halves throughput. Healed Mirror Surgery is 0.66
points below flip averaging (mean of three seeds; for seed 0 a paired bootstrap gives $-0.68$, 95\% interval $-0.87$
to $-0.51$) and needs one pass. There is a trade-off in how the model is stored. Stored densely with its projected
weights, it has the architecture of the original and runs at the same speed, twice the throughput of flip averaging,
but saves no parameters. In its even/odd form it has 23.5\% fewer parameters (and 77\% of the original's FLOPs,
counted analytically), but our implementation of that form runs at 0.72 of the original's throughput, so we make no
speed claim for it; the low-rank baselines are slower than the original in the same benchmark too. Int8 weight
quantisation lowers the accuracy of Mirror Surgery by 0.006 points (original: 0.004), and token
merging~\citep{bolya2023tome} by 0.18 points (original: 0.34); neither preserves exact invariance (flip consistency
99.1\% and 96.8\%).

\paragraph{Downstream tasks.}
The $k$-NN result carries over to every downstream task we tried (Table~\ref{tab:downstream}). With frozen features,
healed Mirror Surgery matches or exceeds healed SVD-LLM on the ImageNet linear probe (80.11 against 79.72), on the four
transfer datasets~\citep{krizhevsky2009learning,cimpoi2014describing,parkhi2012cats,nilsback2008automated} and on ADE20K
segmentation~\citep{zhou2017scene} (47.16 against 46.79 mIoU, 0.43 below the original). These are single runs except
where noted, and the Pets and Flowers gaps are within noise. Under distribution shift, its accuracy drop from ImageNet to
ImageNet-V2~\citep{recht2019imagenet} is 9.17 points against 8.62 for the original: within the one-point margin we
pre-registered, though the paired interval of the difference ($+0.08$ to $+1.02$) shows the drop is larger. On
ImageNet-Sketch~\citep{wang2019learning} Mirror Surgery leads SVD-LLM by 4.2 points. The dataset fills some classes
with flipped and rotated copies of other images, which an invariant model treats alike, but the lead remains after
removing detected near-duplicates (55.03 against 50.43). For segmentation the converted backbone is exactly
equivariant, and a head averaged over the image and its mirror image gives the mirrored mask for all but 422 of the
487.7 million pixels of the 2{,}000 validation images (raw head: 98.98\% of pixels; original model: 95.03\%).

\paragraph{Before healing.}
Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot} shows the conversion without training. Converting the first six blocks costs Mirror Surgery
1.6 points, and the structure is what matters: pairing the same neurons by same-image correlation or by weight
similarity, with everything else unchanged, falls 37 to 49 points below Mirror Surgery at six blocks, and a random
structure destroys the model. This matches the twin statistics: twins respond to mirrored inputs, so on any single
image they are almost uncorrelated (median same-token correlation 0.05), and correlation-based pairing cannot find
them. SVD-LLM, however, is better than Mirror Surgery at every size, by 1.8 points on six MLPs and by 22 points on the
full model. The late blocks, which hold little mirror structure but must use the shared operator, lose most. Mirror
Surgery is therefore a conversion followed by a short distillation, and its value lies in the healed model.

\paragraph{What the even and odd parts encode.}
An exactly invariant feature cannot know which way an object faces, while an odd feature changes sign when the image is
mirrored. In the healed model this splits the representation cleanly (Table~\ref{tab:cub}). On CUB-200-2011
birds~\citep{wah2011caltech}, the even part of the class token identifies the species as well as the full feature (89.47
against 89.42), but a probe trained to read the facing direction from it is at chance: 50.57\% on natural test images
and exactly 50\% on the mirrored test set, as it must be, since every image and its mirror image receive the same
prediction. The odd part, 68 of 768 directions, predicts the facing direction at 99.2\% and the species at 3.7\%. In the
original model the even part still knows the facing direction (86.7\%). Mirror Surgery therefore yields, at no extra
cost, a feature for what is in the image and a separate small feature for which way it is oriented.
